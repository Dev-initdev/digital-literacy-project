# Case Study: UPI / Online Payment Fraud

## Type of Cybercrime
UPI Collect Request Scam (Online Payment Fraud)

---

## The Scenario

Arjun, a first-year engineering student at a university in central India, received a WhatsApp message from an unknown number claiming to be a college senior. The sender said he was setting up a group fund for a departmental event and asked Arjun to help him "test the payment link." He shared a UPI collect request for ₹1 on the student's PhonePe app, saying, "Just approve this tiny amount to verify your account is linked. You'll receive ₹500 as a welcome bonus right after."

Arjun, unfamiliar with how UPI collect requests work, assumed he would be receiving money. He opened the app, saw a request waiting for approval, and tapped "Pay." The ₹1 was deducted from his account.

The sender then said the test failed and asked him to approve a second request for ₹499, claiming that was how the payment system "confirms the reverse transaction." Arjun approved this too. Shortly after, the scammer disconnected and blocked him.

---

## How It Typically Happens (Step by Step)

1. The fraudster contacts the target via WhatsApp, SMS, or social media, posing as a known person, college official, or customer support agent.
2. They build trust by referencing familiar details (college name, batch year, event names).
3. They send a UPI **collect request** — which requires the victim to initiate a payment — and frame it as "receiving" money.
4. The victim approves the request, unknowingly sending money out of their account.
5. The fraudster repeats the request with larger amounts before disappearing.

---

## Who Is Usually Targeted

- First-year students unfamiliar with UPI mechanics
- Students responding to "scholarship" or "refund" announcements online
- Anyone asked to "verify" a payment or "receive" a transfer

---

## Consequences

- Direct financial loss (often ₹500–₹50,000 in repeated transactions)
- Emotional distress and embarrassment
- Potential compromise of linked bank account details
- Loss of trust in legitimate digital payment systems

---

## Source Reference

This is a realistic fictional scenario based on common UPI fraud patterns documented by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) and widely reported by Indian news media.
