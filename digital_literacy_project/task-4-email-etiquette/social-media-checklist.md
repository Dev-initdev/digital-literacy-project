# Social Media Do's and Don'ts for College Students

## ✅ DO's

1. **Keep your profiles professional.** Use a clear, appropriate profile photo and a real name on platforms like LinkedIn and GitHub. Future employers and internship coordinators will search for you.

2. **Think before you post.** Apply the "5-year rule" — ask yourself whether you would be comfortable with a future employer, professor, or family member seeing this post five years from now.

3. **Use strong, unique passwords for each platform.** Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever possible to protect your accounts from unauthorised access.

4. **Give credit when sharing others' content.** If you share an article, image, or idea that belongs to someone else, tag the original creator or source. This builds trust and avoids plagiarism.

5. **Separate personal and professional spaces.** Use LinkedIn for professional updates and achievements. Keep personal opinions and informal conversations to private groups or messaging apps with known friends.

6. **Verify information before sharing.** If you see a shocking or viral news story, check it on two or three credible news sources before resharing. Spreading misinformation — even unintentionally — can damage your reputation.

7. **Report harassment and block abusers.** All major platforms have reporting tools. Use them. Do not engage with trolls or respond to cyberbullying — document it and report it.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

1. **Don't post negative comments about professors, classmates, or institutions publicly.** Even if you feel strongly, public venting can escalate quickly and lead to disciplinary action or permanent reputational damage.

2. **Don't share your location in real time.** Avoid posting your live location, daily schedule, or home address publicly. This is a safety and privacy risk.

3. **Don't accept friend or connection requests from strangers without verification.** Fake profiles are commonly used for phishing, scam, and data harvesting attacks.

4. **Don't use social media as a substitute for official communication.** For academic matters — submitting assignments, requesting extensions, raising concerns — always use your official college email, not WhatsApp or Instagram DMs.

5. **Don't share photos of ID cards, fee receipts, marksheets, or sensitive documents.** These can be misused for identity theft and fraud.
